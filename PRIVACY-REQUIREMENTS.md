RIGHTON — PRIVACY REQUIREMENTS

1. Purpose

RightOn must respect and protect the privacy of every customer.

As an independent digital concierge, RightOn may receive customer requests containing personal details, preferences, locations, financial considerations, and other private information.

The collection and use of this information must be limited, transparent, and controlled.

2. Customer Privacy Principles

RightOn will follow these principles:

- Collect only information necessary to fulfill a request.
- Explain why information is needed when appropriate.
- Never assume permission to use information for unrelated purposes.
- Never sell customer personal information.
- Do not share customer information unnecessarily.
- Give customers meaningful control over their information.
- Retain information only as long as justified.
- Protect customer information throughout its lifecycle.

3. Information Collection

RightOn should collect the minimum information necessary to provide its services.

Information may include:

- Customer requests.
- Location, when relevant to a request.
- Budget or spending preferences, when provided.
- Product or service preferences.
- Contact information, if needed for an account or requested service.
- Saved requests, if the customer chooses to use that feature.

RightOn should not require an account or personal information when a request can reasonably be completed without it.

4. Optional Information

Customers must remain in control of how much information they provide.

The initial request process should remain simple.

When additional information could improve results, RightOn may ask:

"Would you like to enter more information to help us narrow down the results?"

Customers may decline.

Declining to provide optional information should not prevent RightOn from attempting to fulfill the original request when reasonably possible.

5. Use of Customer Information

Customer information may be used only for legitimate purposes connected to the service, including:

- Understanding the customer's request.
- Finding relevant information.
- Comparing products and services.
- Organizing research.
- Improving the customer's results.
- Providing requested account features.
- Maintaining security and preventing abuse.

Information must not automatically become available for unrelated marketing, advertising, profiling, or other purposes.

6. Artificial Intelligence and Outside Services

RightOn may eventually use outside services, including optional AI providers, search providers, and other APIs.

Before sharing customer information with an outside service:

- Evaluate the provider's privacy practices.
- Determine what information the provider receives.
- Review its retention and deletion policies.
- Review whether information may be used for model training or other purposes.
- Avoid sending unnecessary personal information.
- Disclose relevant third-party processing to customers.
- Obtain consent when required.

RightOn should be designed to minimize dependence on outside AI providers.

Customer information should not be sent to an AI provider merely because AI is available.

7. Customer Data Ownership and Control

Customers should have meaningful control over personal information associated with their accounts.

Where applicable, provide the ability to:

- View stored personal information.
- Correct inaccurate information.
- Delete saved information.
- Delete accounts.
- Request a copy of their data.
- Control optional personalization features.

The application must distinguish between customer information and RightOn's original software, systems, and intellectual property.

8. Data Retention and Deletion

RightOn should establish clear retention periods for different categories of information.

Requirements:

- Do not retain information indefinitely without a legitimate reason.
- Delete information when it is no longer needed, subject to legal obligations.
- Establish procedures for account deletion.
- Establish procedures for deleting saved requests.
- Address copies of information held by outside providers.
- Protect backups containing customer information.
- Document exceptions when information must be retained.

9. Location Privacy

Location information should be requested only when it is relevant to the customer's request.

Examples include:

- Finding nearby businesses.
- Comparing local services.
- Identifying regional prices.
- Researching local regulations.

RightOn should not require continuous location tracking to provide ordinary concierge services.

Where possible, customers should be able to enter a city, ZIP code, or general area instead of providing precise location information.

10. Sensitive Information

RightOn should avoid collecting sensitive personal information unless it is genuinely necessary for a requested service.

Examples may include:

- Financial account information.
- Government identification numbers.
- Medical information.
- Authentication credentials.
- Payment card information.

If a service requires sensitive information, additional safeguards and appropriate consent must be established before collection.

11. Advertising and Marketing

Customer requests and private information must not be sold or disclosed to advertisers.

Any future marketing or advertising features must be evaluated separately and must follow applicable privacy requirements.

Personalized advertising must not be introduced without appropriate privacy review and customer controls.

12. Payments

If RightOn eventually processes payments, use an appropriately qualified payment processor.

Avoid storing payment card numbers or security codes directly unless there is a compelling, properly secured, legally compliant reason.

Payment processing should be separated from ordinary request research wherever practical.

13. Privacy Transparency

RightOn should provide an understandable privacy notice explaining:

- What information is collected.
- Why information is collected.
- How information is used.
- Which outside services may receive information.
- How long information is retained.
- How customers can access or delete information.
- How customers can contact RightOn about privacy concerns.

Privacy information should be written in plain language.

14. Legal and Regulatory Compliance

Privacy requirements must be evaluated according to the locations where RightOn operates and the types of information it processes.

Potentially relevant laws may include:

- Applicable United States federal privacy and consumer protection laws.
- State privacy laws.
- Children's privacy requirements, if services are offered to children.
- Payment and financial data requirements, where applicable.
- Other applicable international privacy laws if RightOn serves customers outside the United States.

Legal compliance must be reviewed before launch and as the service expands.

15. Privacy by Design

Privacy must be considered during planning, architecture, development, testing, and expansion.

New features should be reviewed to determine:

- Whether additional information is necessary.
- Whether information can be processed without identifying the customer.
- Whether a third party needs access.
- Whether the information can be deleted.
- Whether the feature creates unnecessary privacy risks.

16. Guiding Principle

RightOn should earn customer trust by collecting less, protecting more, and giving customers control.

The customer should be able to use a digital concierge without surrendering unnecessary personal information.
